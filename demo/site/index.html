MDB Vision · WIUT Hackathon 2026 · Computer Vision track

Every traffic event at a Tashkent intersection, as a time segment — and a warning before a crash.

From one fixed 4K camera we detect and track every road user, align our map of the junction to each video, and turn trajectories into events: jaywalking, vehicles failing to yield, stopped vehicles, stop-line and lane violations, illegal turns, congestion. A causal risk score estimates how likely a crash is in the next 5 s.

Problem and approach

Part A: for each video, return [start, end, label] for 14 event classes, scored by macro F1 over temporal IoU 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 — boundaries matter. Part B: a causal per-frame probability that an accident starts within 5 s. Runs offline on a T4 within 3× the video length.

What is learned, what is rule-based

  • Learned: only the object detectors, YOLO26m (Part A) and YOLO26s (Part B), COCO-pretrained, used as released. No training on the competition footage.
  • Rule-based: tracking, zone alignment, traffic-light reading, every event class and the risk score. Thresholds were chosen on our own frame-level labels of the samples, checked by leaving one video out.
  • Why: the samples contain no accidents and only a few examples of most classes — too little to train a video model, but enough to calibrate transparent rules. Winners of comparable challenges (AI City) also won on trajectory rules and segment post-processing, not on bigger backbones.

The map of the junction

We drew the scene once on a median background: crossings, pavements, islands, lanes with directions, stop line, solid lane dividers, approaches, signal heads. The camera framing shifts by up to ~125 px between recordings, so each video's first frame is matched to the reference with SIFT + RANSAC and the zones are moved by the homography.

Vehicles split into a standing mode (queues, parked cars) and a moving mode; people mostly walk at 0.3-1 heights/s. These ranges set our thresholds: "still" below 0.12-0.15, "walking" 0.2-1.5, faster people are riders.

Findings that shaped the solution

  • The view moves between recordings (up to ~125 px) → zones must be aligned per video (SIFT homography), not hard-coded.
  • Daylight and dusk (C3897 bright; C3902/C3905 dusk with over-exposed red lamps) → the light reader uses lamp position as well as colour.
  • Only two signal heads are readable (pedestrian signal_4, vehicle signal_5, which governs the stop line: 343 of 351 crossings on green).
  • The signal cycle is about 75-80 s (red 33-42 s, green 27-41 s on signal_5), so a queue that clears within one cycle is normal traffic.
  • The traffic pattern is regular: queues form and release with the signal cycle, so "many still cars" is normal and cannot mean congestion or a stopped vehicle by itself.
  • Parked cars stand at the far kerb by the bus stop for whole videos → stopped_vehicle must survive tracker ID switches.
  • Left turns to the bottom-left road come from the leftmost lane — except the two labelled illegal turns.
  • Hundreds of pedestrians use the crossings each video, many walking beside the stripes or between islands → jaywalking and failure_to_yield need careful geometry.

Live demo

Upload an .mp4 from this intersection camera, any size or resolution, up to 2 minutes (longer clips are cut to the first 2 minutes; we scale the video down ourselves, so large 4K files only take longer to upload). Our pipeline runs on the server's CPU — zones aligned to your clip, YOLO26 + ByteTrack, the event rules and the causal risk score — and returns the events, a clickable timeline, an annotated playback and the risk curve. For speed on CPU it uses a smaller detector (YOLO26s for clips up to ~50 s, YOLO26n for longer) and 5 frames per second. On this free 2-core server that takes about 4-5x the clip length (a 30 s clip: about 2 minutes); the progress bar shows each stage and the time left. Other cameras run too, but our zones are drawn for this intersection, so their events are not meaningful.

  1. Upload
  2. Align our zones
  3. Detect and track
  4. Find events
  5. Score risk
  6. Render playback

If the zones sit on the crossings, lanes and islands of your clip, the events are meaningful. If they do not, the clip is from another view and the events should be ignored.

Report

What we built

A detector + tracker + aligned scene map + one transparent rule module per class, a labelling tool with per-video bundles for the team, dev labels for three sample videos, an evaluation harness per class, and a causal pair-risk estimator for Part B. The event layer runs in about 0.04× the video length.

What worked

  • Labelling our own dev set early and scoring every change with the official metric.
  • Designs from the research: overlap-linked static groups, post-encroachment time, origin–destination + lane.
  • Segment post-processing (confirmation, gap bridging, merging) — at tIoU 0.7 boundaries matter most.
  • Not emitting classes we cannot detect reliably.

What did not

  • near_miss from the risk score: the labelled near misses are slow turning conflicts the score does not see.
  • solid_line_crossing: weak until the solid stretches are redrawn.
  • A per-direction congestion test did not beat the simpler junction rule.
  • Part B cannot be validated: no accidents in the samples.

What we would do next

  • Validate Part B and an accident verifier on public CCTV crash clips (ACCIDENT benchmark, TAD).
  • Label more video (the fourth sample) and redraw the solid lines; fine-tune the detector on our frames.
  • A near-miss signal from sudden deceleration or heading change of a turning vehicle next to another.
